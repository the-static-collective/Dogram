# APERTURE-PARTITION-LATTICE-001

## Status
Bounded research specimen. No public operator.

## Question
If a finite state space is observed through declared probe families, what information order is induced by the resulting observational equivalence relations?

## Frozen specimen
Let X={1,2,3,4}. Define two aperture-induced partitions:

P={{1,2},{3,4}}
Q={{1,3},{2,4}}

Interpret a block only as "not distinguished by this declared aperture."

Ordering partitions by refinement (finer = at least as distinguishing):
- P and Q are incomparable.
- Their meet/common refinement is {{1},{2},{3},{4}}.
- Their join/common coarsening is {{1,2,3,4}}.

Thus combining the two apertures separates every supplied state, while retaining only distinctions common to both collapses all four.

Exact Wolfram check returned:
{{{1},{2},{3},{4}}, {{1,2,3,4}}, True, True, True, True}
for meet, join, and the four refinement checks.

## Delta
The previous probe-incomparability frontier need not remain an informal partial order. On a fixed finite state space, each declared aperture induces an equivalence relation/partition. These partitions live in the standard partition lattice. This gives exact finite meet/join calculations without assigning a scalar "strength" to incomparable apertures.

## Provenance
Documented mathematics: equivalence relations on a fixed set correspond to set partitions; partitions ordered by refinement form a lattice. Meet is common refinement (nonempty block intersections). Join is the least common coarsening, equivalently the equivalence closure generated by the two relations.

Project-local inference: finite Dogram probe families can be receipted by their induced partition and compared by refinement, provided the state domain and probe family are explicit.

Speculation: this may become an aperture algebra connecting Dogram to formal concept analysis, abstract interpretation, information orders, and observational semantics. Do not promote without further specimens.
