# COLOR-REFINEMENT-FIXPOINT-001

Frozen specimen: A is C6; B is the disjoint union of two triangles. Both have six vertices and every vertex has degree 2. Starting 1-WL/color refinement from one uniform color, every vertex sees two neighbors of that same color, so neither graph splits: both stabilize immediately with one color class of size six. A has one connected component; B has two, so they are non-isomorphic.

Seal: SAME STABLE COLOR REFINEMENT DOES NOT IMPLY ISOMORPHISM.

This strengthens the equitable-quotient collision from a deliberately coarse quotient to the complete fixpoint of the declared refinement procedure.

Documented mathematics: color refinement is 1-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman and its stable coloring is equitable. Dell, Grohe & Rattan, Lovasz Meets Weisfeiler and Leman (2018), arXiv:1802.08876, connect color-refinement indistinguishability with fractional isomorphism and equality of tree-homomorphism counts. Arvind, Koebler, Rattan & Verbitsky, Graph Isomorphism, Color Refinement, and Compactness (2017), DOI 10.1007/s00037-016-0147-6, study the limits of color refinement as an isomorphism test. Wolfram independently reports C6 as connected and two-regular and lists 2C3 among two-regular graphs.

Refusals: stable refinement is not identity; indistinguishable by 1-WL is not isomorphic; fractional isomorphism is not isomorphism; regularity is not connectivity; structural indistinguishability is not evidentiary equivalence; a fixpoint is not a completeness proof; failure to distinguish is not permission to collapse provenance.
