# MODAL-EXTREMA-INCIDENCE-001

Status: bounded research specimen; authority none; public operator none.

## Question

Does retaining both the existential/may successor union and the representative-common/must intersection determine how successor possibilities are distributed among collapsed representatives?

## Frozen hostile control

Over successor universe `{a,b,c}`, compare representative families:

- L = `{{a,b},{a,c}}`
- R = `{{a},{a,b,c}}`

Both have may union `{a,b,c}` and must intersection `{a}`. Their exact representative successor families differ. Their member-size multisets also expose a residual: L has `(2,2)` while R has `(1,3)`.

Therefore:

`SAME MAY UNION + SAME MUST INTERSECTION != SAME REPRESENTATIVE SUCCESSOR FAMILY.`

The modal extrema retain what at least one representative can do and what every representative can do, but they need not retain how the intermediate possibilities are distributed among representatives.

## Boundary

- MAY SUCCESSOR != OCCURRED SUCCESSOR
- MUST/COMMON SUCCESSOR != INEVITABLE HISTORICAL FUTURE
- REPRESENTATIVE FAMILY != EVIDENCE FAMILY
- SET INCIDENCE != CAUSAL INCIDENCE
- MODAL EXTREMA != COMPLETE BEHAVIOR
- EXACT ONE-STEP FAMILY != BISIMULATION
- STRUCTURAL PRESERVATION != TRUTH OR AUTHORITY

## Next frontier

The easy residual here is member-size multiset. The stronger hostile search is for two distinct representative successor families with the same may union, same must intersection, and same member-size multiset, ideally nonisomorphic under representative and successor relabeling. That would force the surviving delta into incidence geometry rather than simple cardinality.
